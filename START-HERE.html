Section 1 of 8

You already code — the gap is method, not syntax

Nothing here teaches you a language, a framework, or what an LLM is. You use Claude Code every day and you are stuck at chat-driven development: type a request, read a wall of output, paste, repeat. Nine weeks turns that into an engineered loop you design, verify and reuse.

One tool the whole way: Claude Code. Mastery over sampling. Every week ends with artefacts on your own machine — harness files, specs, subagents, transcripts — not notes.

Four disciplines are the spine

Week 1 gives you an hour on each; later weeks go deep on one at a time. Learn the names now, because we use them constantly and you will be expected to speak them.

Context engineering wk 2

What the model sees, at the right scope — global, workspace, project, directory, local.

Harness engineering wk 3

Encode it once instead of repeating it: commands, skills, hooks, permissions.

Loop engineering wk 5–6

Verification that closes the loop, then autonomy you can actually leave running.

Graph engineering wk 7

Work split across agents and sessions, tracked instead of hoped for.

Section 2 of 8

Every week runs through the same three stages, in order

Same shape, nine times. Once you have done week 1, you know how every other week works.

1 · Knowledge

Work through the deck

One self-contained HTML deck per week, split into acts. You read an act, then do its hands-on exercise in Claude Code on a real repo. It ends with a capstone you build yourself.

2 · Application

Ship four to six projects

One GitHub issue per project, each stated like a real ticket: a problem to navigate solo, at the difficulty the deck's capstone rehearsed. Evidence goes on the issue before it counts — repo links, PRs, harness files, transcripts. Stuck, run /guide-me with the issue link: it coaches, never solves.

3 · Reinforcement

Wire it into your daily Claude

The last issue hands you a paragraph for your global ~/.claude/CLAUDE.md. It makes Claude re-surface the week's ideology as TIL lines in your normal work, for months.

The rhythm

A week opens with its deck already on main and its issues already created under one milestone. Do the Knowledge issue first — the project issues assume the deck. There is no deadline: this programme is self-paced, so you move at whatever speed your real work allows. Your trainer reviews the evidence and closes the issues.

Two things never get compressed

Verification loops and reps on a real repo. If you are short on time in a week, cut polish, never those two.

Opening a deck

Do not read decks on GitHub — it renders HTML as raw source. Open the local file after cloning: open weeks/week-1/Knowledge.html on macOS, xdg-open on Linux, or just double-click it. The deck works offline and remembers your progress in that browser on that machine.

Section 4 of 8

Your first hour, in order

  1. Accept the GitHub invitation

    Check the email from GitHub and accept. Tell your trainer your username if you have not already. You get the Triage role — comment and label, no push.

  2. Turn on notifications for the repo

    On the repo page click Watch and choose All Activity. New issues and trainer feedback then reach you instead of sitting unread.

  3. Check your prerequisites

    node 20+, git, a GitHub account, and Claude Code on the Max 5x plan. Confirm the first two now: node -v && git --version. Week 1 wires the rest, including the gh CLI and MCP connectors.

  4. Clone the repo and skim the roadmap

    Clone it, then open roadmap.html. Five minutes. You are looking for the shape of the nine weeks, not the detail.

  5. Open the week 1 milestone

    Go to Milestones and click week 1. Those issues, and only those, are your week.

  6. Start with the Knowledge issue

    The one titled [Week 1] Knowledge. It points at weeks/week-1/Knowledge.html. Read each act, do its hands-on as you hit it, then build the capstone. Do not skip ahead to the project issues.

  7. Then work the Application issues

    Five projects in week 1: wire your environment and have a peer audit it cold, build a capability matrix over ten real tasks, ship one automation and use it three times, annotate a week of your own usage, and write up a loop autopsy. Upload evidence as you go, while you still remember what you did.

  8. Finish with the Reinforcement issue

    Paste the week's paragraph into your global ~/.claude/CLAUDE.md, exactly as the issue describes. This is the step people skip, and it is the one that makes the learning outlast the week.

Section 5 of 8

You finish an issue by commenting and labelling, never by closing it

An issue is written once and shared by the whole cohort. It is not assigned to you and it does not close when you are done. Your progress is a label.

Three steps, every time

  1. Do the work on your own machine, on a real repo.
  2. Comment on the issue with your evidence. Links to your repo or PR, session transcripts, the harness files you wrote, screenshots, output artefacts. Drag attachments straight into the comment box. Two sentences on what you did and what surprised you, plus the artefacts.
  3. Add your own label — shubham:done, akash:done or atishay:done — from the Labels panel on the right of the issue.
What good evidence looks like

Specific and yours. The actual .claude/ files you committed, the actual PR the loop produced, the actual transcript where it failed and what you changed. Generic answers get the label removed with a comment saying what is missing. Redo and re-add it; nobody is keeping score of rework.

Only your trainer closes issues, once every learner's label is on and accepted. An open issue does not mean you are behind — check your own label. To list what you have cleared: 

gh issue list --state all --label "shubham:done"
Section 6 of 8

Four ground rules

You cannot push to this repo, and that is the design

Triage role: comment and label only. Your real work lives in your own repos and your evidence links to it. Clone this one read-only and pull each week for new material.

Go at your own speed

Nothing here has a deadline. When you finish a week, say so on the Knowledge issue and the next one gets built. If you are stuck, say that instead of going quiet.

Use real repos

Week 1 gives you a deliberately flawed practice repo at weeks/week-1/data/gym. From week 3 the projects run on a real codebase you actually work on. Toy examples teach nothing and are obvious to review.

Ask in the open

Stuck on an issue? Comment on that issue. The rest of the cohort is stuck on the same thing and the answer is then useful to all of them.

Section 8 of 8

Stuck?

The deck shows as a wall of code on GitHub. What did I do wrong?

Nothing. GitHub previews HTML as source. Clone the repo and open the local file, or use Download raw file on the file page and open the download in your browser.

I closed my browser mid-deck. Did I lose my progress?

No. Decks persist progress to localStorage on that device. Reopen the same file on the same machine and you pick up where you left off. A different laptop starts fresh.

Can I use Cursor, Copilot or another agent instead?

Not for the projects. The programme is one tool for nine weeks on purpose — the depth comes from not sampling. Use whatever you like outside the projects.

Do I need the Max 5x plan?

Yes. Weeks 1 and 6 treat usage budgeting on that plan as curriculum — windows, weekly caps, model mix, /usage and /context. On a different plan the readings will not match the deck.

My real repo is confidential. Can I still do the real-repo projects?

Yes. Do the work there and submit redacted evidence: describe the change, share the harness files and the session shape, screenshot with names blurred. Never paste client code into an issue. If in doubt, ask on the issue before uploading.

Someone already commented the answer. Can I just agree with it?

No. The projects run on your own environment and your own repos, so two people's evidence should not match. Read theirs after you post yours.

I missed a week. What now?

Do the missed week's Knowledge issue before starting the new week, then catch its projects up in parallel. Say so on the issue so your trainer knows where you are. The weeks stack — week 5 assumes the harness you built in week 3.
