# [Week 2] Application — Write the standing rules for a repo you have never worked in

**Pedagogy:** Application · **Milestone:** Week 2
**Prerequisite:** Week 2 Knowledge issue complete.
**Capstone class:** authoring a scoped layer and measuring it (capstone steps 3 and 4, on a codebase you do not know)

## The Problem

Another learner on this cohort is going to hand you their repository. You have never seen
it. Write the CLAUDE.md that makes sessions in it behave, and they will grade it by
running their own five-task suite with your file and without it.

Writing standing rules for your own repo is easy, because you already know what everyone
keeps getting wrong. Doing it for a stranger's codebase means you have to find that out,
and the only way to find it out is to look: at the conventions the code follows, at what
the tests assume, and at what a session gets wrong on the first attempt.

The grade is not your opinion of the file. It is what their suite does with it.

## Constraints

- Swap both ways with your partner. You write theirs, they write yours.
- You get their repo and their task suite. You do not get a briefing from them, and you
  may not ask them what to write.
- The grading is done by the repo's owner, on their own suite, using
  `weeks/week-2/data/peer-swap-rubric.md`.
- Your file goes at the scope you argue for. Project scope is the obvious answer and it is
  not automatically the right one.

## If nobody is available right now

Use a codebase you have genuinely never worked in. An open-source repo in a language you
know is ideal, and `weeks/week-2/data/relay-workspace` works too.

- Build the five-task suite yourself from the repo's own issue tracker or its obvious
  rough edges, **before** you write the CLAUDE.md.
- Write the file, then A/B it on that suite exactly as the capstone did.
- Grade it against `weeks/week-2/data/peer-swap-rubric.md` yourself.

What you lose is the owner's verdict, which is the sharpest part. What survives is the
core skill: working out what a repo needs when nobody briefs you. Post on the swap board
if you want the real thing later.

- [ ] The file was written without asking the owner what to put in it
- [ ] Graded by the owner on their own suite, both with and without
- [ ] You can name one rule you added because of something you found in their code, not
      because it is a rule you happen to like
- [ ] The scope is argued, not defaulted
- [ ] Evidence uploaded, `<name>:done` label added
