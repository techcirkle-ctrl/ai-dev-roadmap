# [Week 3] Application — You have answered the same permission prompt ninety times this month

**Pedagogy:** Application · **Milestone:** Week 3 · **Due:** Sunday EOD
**Prerequisite:** Week 3 Knowledge issue complete.
**Capstone class:** buying autonomy at the right scope (capstone step 3, from measured data rather than a chosen list)

## The Problem

Every approval you answer costs a couple of seconds, and the seconds are not the real cost.
The real cost is that you have to be at the keyboard. A session that stops fourteen times
cannot run while you are in a meeting, and that limit is set entirely by prompts you have
never once refused.

Measure it, then cut it. The measurement has to come first and it has to be real: which
commands, how often, and how many times you actually said no.

The interesting half is the other direction. Somewhere in that list is something you
approve on reflex that you should be refusing, and something that should never be allowed
at all. Find both.

## Constraints

- A week of real work, counted as it happens. Reconstructing from memory does not count,
  though your shell history is fair evidence.
- Allow only what you have **never** refused. One refusal in the data means it stays a
  prompt, and you say why.
- Deny at least one thing that would genuinely hurt, and argue it.
- Every rule goes at a scope you argue: user, project, or local. Checked-in rules affect
  your teammates, so say what you are asking of them.
- Re-count afterwards on comparable work. Without the second number this is a claim.

## Evidence Required

- [ ] One line: something you had been approving on reflex that you now refuse

- [ ] Before and after counts both exist
