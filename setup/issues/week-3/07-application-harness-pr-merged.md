# [Week 3] Application — Ask a colleague to accept your rules into a repo they also work in

**Pedagogy:** Application · **Milestone:** Week 3
**Prerequisite:** Week 3 Knowledge issue complete.
**Capstone class:** the shared harness (capstone step 5, taken past raising the PR to getting it merged)

## The Problem

A harness on your machine is a personal trick. A harness in the repository is team
infrastructure, and the difference is that somebody else has to agree to live with it.

That is a harder conversation than it sounds. You are asking a colleague to accept rules
that will refuse their edits, commands that assume your conventions, and settings that
change what their sessions do without asking. They are entitled to push back, and their
pushback is the most useful review your harness will ever get.

The capstone raised the pull request. This one gets it merged, or gets it rejected with
reasons you can write down.

## Constraints

- A real repository with a real teammate. Not another learner reviewing as an exercise:
  someone who will live with the result.
- The PR description has to explain what each artifact guarantees and what it costs them.
  A reviewer who has to reverse-engineer your intent will reject it, correctly.
- You must respond to their review by changing something, or by arguing the point in
  writing.
- Merged is the target. Rejected with clear reasons is a valid outcome and needs the same
  evidence.
- Book their time early.

## If nobody is available right now

The reviewer does not have to be another learner. In order of preference:

1. A teammate on the repo, which is the real thing and what the issue is for.
2. Any colleague who works in a repo like yours, reviewing it as a favour.
3. Failing both, open the PR anyway and write the review yourself against the question a
   reviewer would actually ask: for each artifact, what does this refuse, what does it
   cost me, and what do I do when it fires wrongly? Then post the PR link on the swap
   board and leave it open for a real review when someone is free.

Route 3 is the weakest, because the whole point is that somebody else agrees to live with
your rules. Say which route you took and why.
