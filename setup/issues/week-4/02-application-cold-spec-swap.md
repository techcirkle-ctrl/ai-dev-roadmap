# [Week 4] Application — Another learner's session has to build from your spec, with no access to you

**Pedagogy:** Application · **Milestone:** Week 4 · **Due:** Sunday EOD
**Prerequisite:** Week 4 Knowledge issue complete.
**Capstone class:** cold-executability (capstone step 2, run without the ability to patch mid-flight)

## The Problem

You are going on leave on Friday. A feature in your repo has to be built while you are
away, by someone who cannot reach you. All they get is your repository and one document.

Write that document. Then hand it to another learner on this cohort and have their fresh
session attempt the work, with you saying nothing at all while they run it. Every question
their session raises is a question you will not be there to answer.

The number that matters is how many questions it has to ask before it can start, and the
number of decisions it announces it will assume. Silent assumptions count against you more
heavily than questions do: a question is one you would have got to answer, and an
assumption is a decision going past you.

This is the cold-run test from Act 3, run for real. The capstone let you patch the spec and
try again. Here you get one attempt at the version you handed over.

## Constraints

- The spec covers a real feature in a repo you actually work in.
- Your partner runs a genuinely fresh session, in their own environment, with your repo
  and your spec and nothing else. Continuing an existing session invalidates the run.
- You say nothing during their run. No clarifying in chat, no "oh, I meant".
- Swap both ways: you also run a cold session against their spec and report the result.
- Book this early in the weekend. It needs another person's time.

## Evidence Required

Upload on this issue before closing:

- [ ] Your spec, at exactly the version you handed over (a commit link or the file)
- [ ] Their session's output: the numbered questions, and the list of silent assumptions
- [ ] Your post-mortem: each question mapped to the section of the spec that should have

- [ ] Their spec's result when you ran it cold, from the other side of the swap
- [ ] One paragraph: the gap that was obvious to you and invisible in the file

## Success Criteria

- [ ] The handover version of the spec is committed and unedited after the run
- [ ] Both counts are recorded: questions, and silent assumptions
- [ ] Every question is mapped to one of the six spec sections
- [ ] You name your own pattern: the section you habitually under-write
- [ ] The swap ran both ways
- [ ] Evidence uploaded, `<name>:done` label added

Note: zero questions is the target, not the pass mark. A spec that came back with six
questions and a sharp post-mortem beats a vague spec nobody could find fault with.
