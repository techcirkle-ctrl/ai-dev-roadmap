# [Week 4] Application — Approve or reject a plan for work somebody else scoped

**Pedagogy:** Application · **Milestone:** Week 4
**Prerequisite:** Week 4 Knowledge issue complete.
**Capstone class:** plan review (capstone step 3, without the author's context)

## The Problem

A plan lands in front of you for work you did not scope. It reads competently. Every step
is written in confident language, the ordering looks sensible, and nothing in it is
obviously wrong.

Decide whether it ships. You are the last person who sees this before somebody spends a
day building it, and the fix is a paragraph now or a rewrite on Wednesday.

Reviewing your own plan is easy, because you already know what you meant. Reviewing
somebody else's is the real skill, and it is where a written checklist stops being
bureaucracy and starts catching things. Read the plan against the six checks, write a
verdict for every one of them, and send it back with the reasons the failing checks
failed. Do not tell it what to do instead: that hands you your own plan back, which you
cannot review honestly.

## Constraints

- The work must be scoped by someone other than you: a teammate's ticket, another
  learner's spec from the swap, or one of the four tickets in
  `weeks/week-4/data/vague-tickets.md`.
- All six verdicts get written down, including the ones that pass. Writing the passes is
  what makes the failures visible.
- You send it back with reasons only, never with instructions.
- If the first plan genuinely survives all six checks, say so and defend it. That is a
  valid outcome and it needs an argument, not a shrug.

- [ ] The rejection names failing checks and reasons, and contains no instructions
- [ ] You can name one concrete difference between the two plans, or argue convincingly
      that the first plan was already sound
- [ ] The work being planned was scoped by someone other than you
